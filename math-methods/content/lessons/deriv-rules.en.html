KEY IDEAS · §§7.2–7.3

Follow each changing part

Economic quantities often combine other quantities. Revenue multiplies price by output; average cost divides cost by output. Differentiation rules track how every part changes.

$$ (uv)'=u'v+uv', \qquad (u/v)'=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}, \qquad \frac{dz}{dx}=\frac{dz}{dy}\frac{dy}{dx}. $$

For example, if inverse demand is $P(Q)=26-2Q$, revenue is $R(Q)=P(Q)Q$, so $MR=P+QP'=26-4Q$. The product rule counts both the change in quantity and the change in price. Level 3 revisits this result using elasticity from later material. For $AC=C(Q)/Q$ with $Q>0$, the quotient rule gives $AC'=(MC-AC)/Q$: marginal cost above average cost means average cost is rising.

The chain rule follows a sequence such as labor $L\to$ output $Q\to$ revenue $R$: $dR/dL=(dR/dQ)(dQ/dL)$. If $y=f(x)$ is locally invertible and $f'(x)\ne0$, its inverse slope is $dx/dy=1/f'(x)$. Check monotonicity before using the inverse rule.

Watch for: multiplying two derivatives instead of adding the product-rule terms; reversing the quotient numerator; omitting the inner derivative.
