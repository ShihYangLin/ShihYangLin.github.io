DERIVATIVE BASICS · §§6.2–6.3, 7.1

From a finite change to a marginal effect

Motivation

Comparative statics asks how an outcome responds when a parameter changes. For a small but finite change, we can measure the average response. Shrinking that change gives an instantaneous rate, such as the marginal cost of producing a little more output.

Key ideas

$$\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\quad(h\ne0),\qquad f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$$

The quotient is the slope of a secant line joining two points. As $h$ approaches zero, its limit is the tangent slope. The quotient depends on both $x$ and $h$; the derivative function depends on $x$ alone.

$$\frac{d}{dx}c=0,\qquad \frac{d}{dx}(ax^n)=anx^{n-1},\qquad \frac{d}{dx}(u+v)=u'+v'$$

A constant has no response to $x$. The power rule gives a quick way to find the slope without rebuilding a limit each time, including for negative and fractional powers where the domain permits them. Differentiate a function first, then evaluate it at a point.

$$MC(Q)=\frac{dC}{dQ},\qquad y-f(x_0)=f'(x_0)(x-x_0)$$

Marginal cost is the slope of the total-cost curve when output is treated as continuous. The same derivative supplies the slope for a tangent line through $(x_0,f(x_0))$.

Worked examples

Difference quotient before the limit

Let $f(x)=4x^2+3x+7$. Expanding $f(x+h)$ and subtracting $f(x)$ leaves $8xh+4h^2+3h$. Divide by $h\ne0$ to get $8x+4h+3$. As $h\to0$, the derivative is $f'(x)=8x+3$.

Tangent and marginal cost

Suppose $C(Q)=18+5Q+2Q^2$. Then $MC(Q)=5+4Q$, so at $Q=3$ the tangent slope is $17$. Since $C(3)=51$, its tangent is $C=51+17(Q-3)$.

A fractional power

For $x>0$, let $f(x)=6\sqrt{x}+4x$. Rewrite the root as $6x^{1/2}$, apply the power rule, and obtain $f'(x)=3x^{-1/2}+4=3/\sqrt{x}+4$.

Common mistakes

Do not set $h=0$ before cancelling it. Do not confuse the derivative function with its value at one point. Keep the fixed-cost derivative at zero, and respect $x>0$ for fractional or negative-power examples here.
