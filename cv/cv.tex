\section*{\MakeUppercase{Working Paper}}

\begin{enumerate}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“Consumption Weighting and Inflation Inequality across Household Groups: Evidence from Malawi.”} Coauthored with Angela Seyama and Chia-Yu Hung. \textit{Under review}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] Households with different consumption baskets can face different inflation exposure even under common national category price changes, which matters when transfers and poverty lines are adjusted using a single index. Using three waves of Malawi's Integrated Household Survey, we compare six household-group contrasts over a moderate-inflation period in 2016--2019 and a high-inflation period in 2019--2024 under equal-household democratic and expenditure-weighted plutocratic aggregation. Under the household-oriented democratic measure, bottom-quintile, rural, below-primary-education, and female-headed households have higher exposure in both periods. Plutocratic aggregation widens all four gaps because higher-spending households within groups generally hold baskets exposed to slower price growth, and this gradient is weaker among the more exposed groups. It also reverses the age comparison in both periods. Food accounts for most of the expenditure, residence, and education gaps, while housing, water and energy is central to female headship gap. Thus, the main socioeconomic pattern is robust, but gap magnitudes and some demographic conclusions depend on how households are weighted. The aggregation rule should therefore be stated when reporting group inflation.\\ doi:\href{https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10878722/v1}{10.21203/rs.3.rs-10878722/v1}
    \end{itemize}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“Heterogeneous Spillovers of Quantitative Tightening in Taiwan.”} \textit{Preparing for submission}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] \emergencystretch=1.5em This paper examines heterogeneity within the international spillovers of U.S. quantitative tightening (QT). Using high-frequency LSAP surprises and regime-dependent local projections, I study Taiwan, a trade-dependent small open economy whose monetary statistics permit an accounting decomposition of M2. Across QT phases, Full-Reinvestment produces the strongest short-run bond-market response and is followed by contractions in industrial production, real exports, and manufacturing employment. In monetary aggregates, Full-Reinvestment is expansionary, whereas Asset-Runoff is weak to negative, generating a large Asset-Runoff minus Full-Reinvestment contrast. The Asset-Runoff-side weakness in M2 coincides with a fall in the banking system's net foreign assets and is amplified by local financial uncertainty. These findings show that QT spillovers are regime-dependent and cannot be fully captured by immediate asset-price reactions.
    \end{itemize}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“The Labor Supply of Married Women and Spousal Tax Deductions in Japan.”} Coauthored with Minchung Hsu, Pei-Ju Liao, and Yi-Chan Tsai.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] Population aging results in the problem of labor shortage. The labor force of Japanese females presents a solution to the Japanese government. Despite measures to improve the female labor force participation rate, encouraging women to work more or even shift from part-time jobs to full-time jobs was considered by the government. This study employs a standard incomplete markets model (or the Bewley-Huggett-Aiyagari style model) with heterogeneous households to examine the impact of two tax reforms (2004 and 2018) and a counterfactual reform abolishing spousal deduction on women's employment. Findings indicate that reducing spousal deduction after the 2004 tax reform boosts labor participation due to negative wealth effects, while the 2018 income threshold extensions result in a shift from full-time to part-time employment. Conversely, completely abolishing spousal deduction encourages a shift from part-time to full-time employment.
    \end{itemize}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“A Two-Stage Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood Method for Poisson Models with Two Binary Endogenous Explanatory Variables.”} Coauthored with Chingmu Chen, Shin-Kun Peng, and Wen-Jen Tsay.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] This paper introduces a novel estimator for the Poisson model with dual binary endogenous explanatory variables, addressing the need to quantify the effects of preferential economic integration agreements. We develop a two-stage pseudo-maximum likelihood estimator and derive analytical gradient matrices as well as Hessian matrices. These exact formulas improve computational speed significantly. Applying our approach to trade data, we find that preferential trade agreements have a positive impact on bilateral trade flows, while bilateral investment treaties have a negative effect. Additionally, the positive interaction term between these two treaties suggests that preferential trade agreements promote horizontal foreign direct investment.
    \end{itemize}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“Robust Inference for Interrupted Time Series Models with Serially Dependent Disturbances.”} Coauthored with Wen-Jen Tsay and Hsin-Chieh Wong. \textit{Under review}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] This paper considers inference problems related to the interrupted time series (ITS) model, which is used as a quasi-experimental approach for evaluating policy intervention effects over time. Under a single-treatment-group design with two interventions and serially correlated errors, we establish the asymptotic distribution of the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimators, which involves Brownian motion functionals and reveals superconsistency for the trending variables when the estimation error is properly rescaled. Interestingly, the asymptotic covariance matrix of the rescaled estimator is proved to be invertible, even though the convergence rates of the OLS estimator for the level and trending variables differ, and the trending variables have different observation lengths. We further develop a studentized sieve bootstrap procedure and establish its asymptotic validity. In simulation experiments, the proposed bootstrap reduces the size distortion of $t$-tests based on standard normal critical values in every configuration considered.\\ doi:\href{https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5486489}{10.2139/ssrn.5486489}
    \end{itemize}
    \Needspace{5\baselineskip}
    \item \textbf{“A Two-Step Poisson Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimator for Structural Poisson Models with Multiple Binary Endogenous Regressors.”} Coauthored with Wen-Jen Tsay.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[] This paper studies structural Poisson models for count outcomes with multiple endogenous binary regressors. While the existing literature mainly considers cases with one or two endogenous binary variables, we extend the models to allow for $k$ binary endogenous explanatory variables and develop a two-step Poisson quasi-maximum likelihood estimator for this setting. We show that the conditional mean admits a representation as a ratio of multivariate normal probabilities, which makes the structural model estimable in practice. We then derive analytical score and Hessian formulae of the corresponding likelihood function from recursive multivariate normal distribution derivatives and establish a Murphy-Topel-type asymptotic variance formula for valid inference. Monte Carlo results show that the proposed estimator can substantially reduce bias in the main structural coefficients relative to naive Poisson quasi-maximum likelihood when endogeneity is strong.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
